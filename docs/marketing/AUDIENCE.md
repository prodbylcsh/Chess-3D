# Who Wizard Chess is for

This is the base for all marketing: posts, ads, copy, the shop's offers, and the order in
which features get promoted. Read it before writing anything aimed at players. Change it
when real numbers (sign-ups, purchases, ad results) show something different.

**Rule:** marketing describes the **finished platform**: everything on the roadmap
(`docs/PLATFORM.md` §9) counts as built: ranked with seasons, tournaments, replays, more
piece sets, boards and effects. We start posting once it is built, but we plan now.

How sure we are: market numbers come from the sources at the end (most are platform or
industry estimates, not independent research). The personas are **reasoned assumptions**
built on those numbers. They are hypotheses to test with ads and sign-up data, not facts.

**The one-line promise:** *Chess where the pieces really fight, with a ranked ladder
you already understand.*

**What nobody else has together:** cinematic 3D captures **+** a League-style ranked ladder

in the browser. Chess.com and Lichess are the best places to play serious chess, but they
look like tools. 3D chess apps in the stores look like games, but they are single-player
toys without a real ladder. We sit between them.

## 2. The market in numbers

- **Online chess is big and young.** Chess.com: 100 M members in 2022, 200 M in 2025,
  ~250 M in early 2026; ~10 M daily active users in 2026 (+26% a year); record of 10 M
  active members on one day in January 2023. About 1.5 M pay. The biggest growth in 2023
  came from players aged 13–17.
- **Who plays online:** chess.com's web audience is about 66% male, 34% female, largest
  group 25–34 (Similarweb, 2026). An older chess.com breakdown: under 18 5%, 18–24 34%,
  25–34 31%, 35–44 15%, 45–54 8%, 55+ 7%. About 95% of players are rated **under 1400**:
  the typical online player is a casual, not a strong club player.
- **Why the boom:** lockdowns, *The Queen's Gambit*, and streamers (GothamChess, Hikaru,
  chess.com's events with non-chess influencers). Commentators say streaming made chess
  less stuffy; by 2026 the press calls chess "young and hip" (chess bars, chess clubs as
  social events).
- **Gamers:** the average gamer is about 36 (ESA). PC players ~936 M, mobile ~3 B (Newzoo).
- **Discovery happens on social media.** Gen Z and millennials find brands on Instagram and
  TikTok more than through search engines (eMarketer, Sprout Social: 41% of Gen Z turn to
  social first). Instagram search reads the name, username and bio.
- **Ranked psychology:** visible status tiers (Bronze, Silver, Gold…) motivate more than
  numbers; people put in more effort as a threshold gets close (goal-gradient effect, seen in
  badge systems); fear of losing a status is stronger than the wish to gain one. A study of
  League of Legends players links rank to validation-seeking and positive emotion (and to
  toxicity: moderate the chat).
- **Why players buy cosmetics:** to be unique, to be seen, for the looks, and as status
  ("I earned this", "I'm good"). Fortnite and Valorant studies show the same. Our items show
  to the opponent in every game, so they work as status.
- **Why people keep playing:** self-determination theory (Ryan, Rigby, Przybylski 2006):
  games that feel like **competence** (I'm getting better), **autonomy** (my choice, my
  style) and **relatedness** (friends, community) are enjoyed and played again.
- **Habits:** Duolingo's retention team saw retention rise once a streak reaches 7 days
  (loss aversion), and leagues bring players back to defend their place. Streaks can turn
  into pressure, so shields (we have them) are part of a healthy design.

## 3. The primary audience: "The gamer who knows chess"

**In one sentence:** a man in his late twenties or thirties who grew up on video games,
knows how chess works (maybe played it as a kid or picked it up in 2020–2023), plays casually
on his phone or PC, and finds chess platforms dull while missing the thrill of ranked games.

Persona name for the team: **"Adam, 31"**.

### Who he is

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Gender | mostly men (about 70%); women ~30% (chess.com's audience is 66/34) |
| Age | 24–38 (core 27–34) |
| Where | Europe and the English-speaking world; Czechia and Slovakia first for word of mouth (we speak Czech) |
| Education and mind | secondary or university; analytical, likes systems, rules and getting better at something; sees himself as clever; not necessarily strong at chess (rated 600–1400 on chess.com, if at all) |
| Work | office and knowledge jobs: IT, engineering, finance, sales, logistics, students finishing university; some skilled trades |
| Money | stable income, middle class (assumption: CZ net 35–60k CZK a month, Western Europe €2,000–3,500); spends **€15–50 a month** on entertainment subscriptions (Netflix, Spotify, Game Pass/PS Plus), has bought skins or a battle pass before; spending €5–20 on a hobby is not a decision he agonises over |
| Devices | iPhone or Android all day, a PC or laptop in the evening, maybe a console |
| Gaming past | grew up on PlayStation, CS, LoL, WoW, FIFA, Clash Royale; knows MMR, divisions, seasons, skins and battle passes without explanation |
| Media | Instagram and YouTube daily, Reels and Shorts in idle minutes, Reddit, Twitch now and then, podcasts on the way to work |
| Chess media | has watched GothamChess or Hikaru clips, *The Queen's Gambit*, chess memes ("Google en passant") |

### A normal day

- 6:45 alarm, phone first: Instagram, news, messages.
- Commute or coffee: 10 minutes of scrolling. **Here a Reel of a knight smashing a piece
  stops him.**
- Work: screens all day, meetings, small breaks (5 minutes: phone).
- Lunch: 20 minutes, half of it on the phone.
- Evening: gym or friends twice a week; otherwise dinner, a series, maybe a game. Less time
  for 40-minute LoL matches than at 20; a toxic team ruins the evening.
- 22:30 in bed with the phone: "one more game".

### His problems in life (what he would not say aloud)

- **Not enough time,** but plenty of dead minutes he fills with scrolling, and he feels bad
  about it ("I waste my evenings").
- **Stress and a head full of work;** wants something that takes him out of it completely
  for a while (flow), not more passive content.
- **Missing the feeling of progress.** At work progress is slow and invisible; in games he
  used to see it (levels, ranks). He misses "I'm getting better at something".
- **Competitive itch without the cost.** Ranked team games take 30–40 minutes, depend on
  four strangers and get toxic. He wants to compete, but alone and in short sessions.
- **Guilt about gaming.** Games feel childish to his partner or to himself; chess feels like
  a *respectable* game, "good for the brain".
- **Friends drifting apart.** Fewer chances to do something with friends; a quick game over a
  link keeps a friendship going.

### Why someone like him plays chess at all

- **Competence:** pure skill, no luck, no team. Losing is his own fault, and winning is his
  own merit. Improvement is real and measurable.
- **Short and flexible:** a game is 5–15 minutes, a puzzle 1 minute, fits the dead minutes.
- **Status and self-image:** "I play chess" sounds clever; beating a friend feels great.
- **A "good" screen habit:** brain training instead of doomscrolling.
- **The scene is cool now:** streamers, memes, chess bars.

### Why *this* chess and not chess.com or Lichess

| What he wants | chess.com / Lichess | Wizard Chess |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Feels like a video game | flat 2D board, looks like a tool | cinematic 3D: pieces leap, smash, cast spells; checkmate is a scene |
| A ladder he understands | a number (Elo 1137) | Iron → Challenger, divisions, seasons, rewards: the language of LoL and Valorant |
| Something to show off | little (a few themes) | piece sets, boards and effects the opponent sees every game; season icons and badges nobody can buy |
| Progress that feels like a campaign | puzzle rush, lists | worlds of 100 puzzles on a map, a bonus every 10, an item for finishing |
| No shame as a beginner | competitive, judgemental | Learn from zero on the 3D board, play the AI, ranks start at Silver for everyone |
| Easy with friends | needs accounts | one link, opens in the browser, no account |
| Fair | strong anti-cheat | every game analysed; coins never buy an advantage |

### What he pays for (and at what price)

1. **Cosmetics** (status, looks): a piece set or effect he saw an opponent use. Coin packs
   €4.99–€19.99. Trigger: "I want that set" after a loss or a cool game.
2. **Premium Wizard €9.99** if he plays several times a week: monthly coins (a set's worth
   over two months), every puzzle world, unlimited Game Review. Trigger: the third time he
   hits a paywall in puzzles or Review.
3. **Puzzle worlds / Learn** with coins if he prefers to own things.

### Objections and how we answer them

| He thinks | We show |
| --- | --- |
| "Gimmick: nice animation, bad chess" (the old *Battle Chess* complaint) | real rules, real ladder, Stockfish analysis, fair-play checks; the animations never cost clock time |
| "Another app to install" | browser, phone and PC, nothing to install |
| "Pay to win?" | coins buy looks only; ranked and coins are decided on the server |
| "I'm bad at chess" | you start at Silver II with others like you; Learn and puzzles from zero |
| "Nobody plays it" | (after launch) live counts, spectating, friends via link; never fake numbers |

### Where we reach him

- **Instagram Reels and Feed** (interests: chess, chess.com, Magnus Carlsen, GothamChess,
  League of Legends, Valorant, Steam, PlayStation; age 22–40; men and women, the creative
  aims at men first).
- **YouTube Shorts and TikTok** with the same clips.
- **Reddit** (r/chess, r/chessbeginners, r/AnarchyChess memes) with honest posts by the
  developer.
- **Chess creators** with a casual, gamer audience (micro-creators of 10–100k first).

### How we talk to him

- **Tone:** confident, a little epic, gamer slang he knows (ranked, tier, grind, GG),
  never childish, never "brain gym" preaching.
- **Hooks that fit:** "Chess, but the pieces actually fight." "Iron to Challenger." "Your
  knight deserves better than a 2D square." "Mate in 2. Black just took your queen."
- **Avoid:** "best chess app ever" (unbelievable), Harry Potter names (trademark), fake
  urgency, anything that suggests money can win games.

### Why he is the easiest group

1. **He already knows the rules:** no teaching before the fun starts.
2. **He already speaks "ranked":** Iron to Challenger needs no explanation.
3. **He already buys cosmetics and subscriptions:** the business model feels normal to him.
4. **He has money of his own** (unlike teenagers) and decides alone.
5. **He is easy to target:** clear interests (chess + gaming) on Meta and YouTube.
6. **He brings friends:** the link game turns one player into two.

### How we measure that it works

Ad click-through on chess+gaming interests, sign-up rate from Instagram, share of players
who reach 10 games, the first coin pack within 30 days, Wizard trials.

## 4. Alternative audience A: Gen Z ranked gamers (16–24)

**In one sentence:** young League of Legends, Valorant, CS2, Clash Royale and Brawl Stars
players who think chess is for old people, until they see a ranked ladder, skins and pieces
that explode.

Persona: **"Matěj / Jayden, 19"**.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Gender | ~75% male |
| Age | 16–24 (ads only to 18+; younger ones arrive through friends and organic posts) |
| Money | little: pocket money, a student job, €5–15 a month for games; buys a battle pass or a skin, rarely a subscription |
| Life | school or university, part-time job, evenings on Discord with friends, games in groups |
| Mind | quick, competitive, sensitive to "cringe", follows streamers, lives in memes |
| Problems | pressure to perform (school, social media comparison), boredom, a need to belong and to be good at something visible; ranked anxiety in team games |

**Why chess for them:** a 1v1 where nobody else can lose it for you; short games; their
favourite streamers play it; "chess is having a moment" in their feed.

**Why our chess:** it looks and feels like *their* games: tiers, divisions, seasons, skins,
effects, a Challenger rank to chase, quick 5+3 Blitz, a friend's link in Discord. The
cinematic captures make clips worth sharing.

**What they pay for:** the €4.99 pack, sometimes €9.99; season rewards matter more than
purchases (they grind instead of paying). Their value is **reach**: they share clips, invite
friends and fill the matchmaking queues, which keeps the ladder alive for paying players.

**Hooks:** "Chess has ranks now." "Iron → Challenger. Where do you land?" "POV: your
knight hits a pawn." "Hardstuck Silver?" Short Reels, memes, rank reveals, "comment your
rank".

**Care needed:** minors. Meta lets advertisers reach teens by age and location only, and the
EU's Digital Services Act forbids personalised ads to minors, so paid ads go to 18+; our
Terms need a parent's consent under 18. Never push coin purchases or coin stakes at
this group; talk about play, ranks and earning items.

**Where:** TikTok, Reels, YouTube Shorts, Discord communities, gaming meme pages, Twitch
co-streams.

## 5. Alternative audience B: fantasy fans and collectors (25–45)

**In one sentence:** people who grew up with wizard's chess in films and books, *Battle
Chess* on the PC or fantasy board games, and want chess to feel like magic, not like a
spreadsheet.

Persona: **"Klára, 34"** (this is the group where women are closest to half).

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Gender | about 50/50 |
| Age | 25–45 (the "Harry Potter generation", born 1985–2000; *Battle Chess* nostalgia for 35+) |
| Money | stable; spends on hobbies with love: board games, LEGO, collector editions, Steam sales, D&D books; pays for beauty and craft |
| Life | full-time job, maybe young kids, evenings with series, board-game nights with friends, cozy games (Stardew Valley, Hogwarts Legacy) |
| Mind | imaginative, aesthetic, loves stories and worlds; plays chess casually or wants to |
| Problems | little time "for herself", wants something beautiful and calm in the evening, escapism; feels chess platforms are cold and male |

**Why chess for them:** the dream of the magic chess game they saw as children; a quiet,
smart game for the evening; something to play with a partner or a kid.

**Why our chess:** the candlelit scene, magic bolts, shattering marble, collectable sets
and boards, puzzle worlds with their own landscapes, Learn that teaches gently. The
platform is beautiful, and collecting looks is part of the game.

**What they pay for:** **cosmetics** (they are the collectors: Monolith sets, boards,
effects; the Archmage Wardrobe), Learn and puzzle worlds. Highest spend per player of all
groups if the catalogue keeps growing.

**Hooks:** "The chess game you dreamed of as a kid." "Every capture is a little spell."
"Collect your set." Slow, beautiful close-ups, ASMR-like sound, the candlelit study,
behind-the-scenes of how a set was made.

**Care needed:** never use Harry Potter names, images or quotes (trademark); say "wizard's
duel", "magic", "spell" instead.

**Where:** Instagram (aesthetic Reels, carousels), Pinterest, YouTube, fantasy and
board-game communities, cozy-gaming creators.

## 6. Alternative audience C: beginners and self-improvers (22–45)

**In one sentence:** adults who never learned chess properly (or forgot it) and want a smart
habit instead of doomscrolling, the people who keep a Duolingo streak.

Persona: **"Eva, 29"** (or "Petr, 38, wants to play with his son").

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Gender | about 50/50, more women than any other group |
| Age | 22–45 |
| Money | middle income; already pays for self-improvement apps (Duolingo, Headspace, Blinkist, a gym) |
| Life | a busy job, wants to "use" her free time well; parents who want to play with their kids |
| Mind | curious, ambitious, a little afraid of looking stupid; likes clear steps and visible progress |
| Problems | screen-time guilt, the wish to keep the brain sharp, impostor feelings in "clever" hobbies, chess platforms feel intimidating and full of strong men |

**Why chess for them:** "I always wanted to learn it", a brain workout, playing with a
child or partner, the chess boom made it cool.

**Why our chess:** Learn starts at zero on a beautiful 3D board (explain → show → try →
check), mistakes are explained by the rule, not by an engine; daily puzzle and streak with
shields like Duolingo; vs AI and friends before strangers; ranks start at Silver for
everyone. It is friendly, not judgemental.

**What they pay for:** **Premium** (Apprentice €4.99 or Wizard €9.99) or Learn for
75,000 coins (≈ €20). They are subscription people: if the habit sticks, they stay for
months.

**Hooks:** "Never learned chess? 10 minutes a day." "From 'how does the knight move?' to
your first checkmate." "Day 7 of your streak." Step-by-step carousels (saved and shared),
before/after progress, the daily puzzle.

**Care needed:** keep it kind; no "only 1% can solve this" (it scares them away); show
women and parents in creatives, not only the epic side.

**Where:** Instagram carousels and Reels, Pinterest ("learn chess"), YouTube
("how to play chess" searches), Facebook for parents.

## 7. How the groups fit together

| | Primary: gamer who knows chess | A: Gen Z ranked | B: fantasy & collectors | C: beginners |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Main draw | 3D captures + ranked | ranked + skins + clips | magic look + collecting | Learn + streaks |
| Pays through | cosmetics, Wizard | small packs, little | cosmetics, Archmage | Premium, Learn |
| Value for us | revenue + players | reach + full queues | highest spend per player | long subscriptions |
| Ease | easiest | easy to reach, hard to monetise | needs beautiful content | needs teaching content |

**Order:** start with the primary group (it pays and needs no explanation), let Gen Z come
through the same ranked and capture content (it spreads it), add fantasy content for
collectors once the catalogue has several sets, and grow beginners with Learn carousels
(they are evergreen and saved).

**Content mix (first months):** 40% capture spectacle (everyone), 25% ranked and seasons
(primary + A), 20% puzzles and learning (C + primary), 15% items and collecting (B +
primary).
